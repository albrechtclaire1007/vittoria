II · La vostra identità

Vittoria Conti

You are a talented art historian and restoration specialist whose eye for paper, ink, paint and provenance has recently brought you to Tenuta Antonelli–Mastropietro. Tonight’s rediscovery of the Riserva 1947 interests you not only as a historic object, but because old labels and documents can tell a story of their own.

You are observant, precise and quietly curious. You notice small inconsistencies other people overlook, but you do not jump to conclusions. Your expertise makes you especially useful when a document, label or old piece of printed material needs to be interpreted.

Perché siete qui

You have been invited because of your work with historic material at the Tenuta and because Clarissa values your judgement. The rediscovered bottle may prove to be as interesting as the papers surrounding it.

Come vestirsi

Elegant late-1960s Italian evening wear with an artistic, cultivated edge: a refined dress or tailored ensemble in wine, olive, cream, camel, black or muted jewel tones. Sophisticated rather than theatrical.

IV · In confidenza

Da leggere con discrezione

You know from your work that an old document can be perfectly genuine and still be used to support a false story. Among estate materials you have seen an early Antonelli label proof whose wording differs slightly from the final printed version.

If old labels, photographs or papers appear during the evening, look at them carefully. Your task is not to accuse anyone, but to explain what the material can—and cannot—prove. Pay particular attention to whether a label is a preliminary proof or something that would actually have appeared on a finished bottle.

Da ricordare

You have personally seen the early Antonelli label proof and are confident it was preliminary material, not a finished bottle label. When the genuine rediscovered bottle is presented, compare its printed details with what you remember. Explain the distinction when it becomes relevant, but do not use your expertise to accuse someone without supporting evidence.
